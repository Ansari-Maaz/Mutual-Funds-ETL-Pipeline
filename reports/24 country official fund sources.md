# Free official sources can close most Twelve Data gaps

Official, free sources can make the pipeline's fund data resemble Twelve Data's across the 24 in-scope countries, but the lift is uneven and mostly lives in identity, NAV for a subset, fees and holdings for a few markets. The biggest verified levers are: **France's AMF GECO per-share API (daily NAV back to 2004, net assets, coupons, no key, about 20k share classes)**, the **ESMA FIRDS + HMRC reporting-funds + ESMA cross-border register + GLEIF stack (ISIN, LEI, manager, status, inception for IE and LU)**, **Germany's BaFin CSV plus the Bundesanzeiger evening price CSV**, **Russia's Bank of Russia register and data showcase (a country with 0 funds today, 4,025 non-excluded registered funds, 673 retail funds with ISIN, fees, benchmark and returns)**, the **Swiss FINMA XLSX**, and **issuer files for Australian ETFs**. Brazil holdings are blocked only by network access (CVM returns 403 to this host), not by code. No free official bulk NAV history exists for DE, IT (beyond a 68-fund rolling file), NL, DK, SE, CH, GB, IE, LU, CA, MX, ZA, SG, AE or the unlisted funds of HK, CN, TW, JP and KR; for those the fallback is forward-building history from daily snapshots, issuer or manager files, or a licence. Twelve Data's ratings, category ranks, ESG scores, valuation metrics and brokerages are proprietary and should be returned as unavailable. Everything below is labelled VERIFIED (fetched during the 2026-11-17/18 research), UNVERIFIED (search snippet or background only) or UNOFFICIAL. Several researchers stopped before exhausting their budget, so the final section lists what was not checked.

### How much each source family really delivers

The European identity stack is the highest-yield item. The ESMA FIRDS "C" file carries 21,076 unique ISINs with LU 7,126 and IE 6,149, each with issuer LEI, but only venue-traded share classes ([ESMA FIRDS index](https://registers.esma.europa.eu/solr/esma_registers_firds_files/select?q=file_name:FULINS_C*&sort=publication_date+desc&rows=3&wt=json&fl=file_name,publication_date,download_link)). The HMRC list adds unlisted share classes (**105,267 unique valid ISINs, LU 59,502, IE 38,656, all-time including ceased**) ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/offshore-funds-list-of-reporting-funds)). ESMA's cross-border register adds manager, LEI, status and creation date for 13,611 LU and 5,828 IE UCITS but no ISIN or currency ([ESMA cbdif](https://registers.esma.europa.eu/solr/esma_registers_funds_cbdif/select?q=funds_domicile_cou_code:LU+AND+funds_legal_framework_name:UCITS&fq=entity_type:funds_report&rows=100&wt=json)). Together they give identity for LU/IE without paid data; none gives NAV history, fees, risk or holdings. The same Bank of Spain CSVs already feeding DE, IT, NL, DK and LU hold only code, LEI, name, manager, address and a policy label.

France is the opposite: the missing NAV is a crawl, not a licence. In a 25-compartment probe, 26 of 34 shares returned NAV history (about 75%), with a sample series of 5,532 daily dates from 2004-09-24 to 2026-11-13, assets under management in thousand EUR and coupons from 2005 ([GECO API](https://geco.amf-france.org/back-office/funds/vlsCouponsAndOst/sh29449?startDate=1990-01-01&endDate=2026-11-17)). The research found no stated reuse licence for GECO, so attribution, throttling and a written confirmation from AMF are prudent steps.

The listed-fund exchanges give breadth but not NAV: the Euronext funds directory returned 87 XAMS funds and zero Paris funds, and trading price must be stored as a market price, not NAV ([Euronext funds list](https://live.euronext.com/en/products/funds/list)). SIX lists 2,334 ETFs (IE 1,617, LU 472, CH 67) with the reuse caveat in the licensing table ([SIX fqs](https://www.six-group.com/fqs/ref.json?select=ISIN&where=ProductLine=IF)).

## Where no free official bulk source exists, and the fallback

The fallback pattern is the same across countries: build history forward from a daily official snapshot, or attach issuer/manager files, and label the source tier. The cases are listed explicitly because several are easy to overstate.

Germany has no official bulk NAV history. The fallback is the Bundesanzeiger evening CSV: it is downloadable from 18:00 and contains only that day's prices (404 rows on 2026-11-17, German number format, needs a session cookie from the Fondsdata page first), so history only exists from the first stored day; the "historical price data" page per fund is UNVERIFIED and paid packages exist ([Fondsdata how it works](https://www.bundesanzeiger.de/pub/en/howto-fondsdata)). France has official history, but no official holdings, fees, SRI or benchmark in the JSON seen; those sit in KID PDFs behind GECO `document/*` endpoints that returned 400 and are UNVERIFIED. Italy's Borsa Italiana file is a rolling window of about 30 days over 68 ISINs, so the connector must upsert it on every run and accumulate ([Borsa Italiana monthly file](https://www.borsaitaliana.it/borsa/atf/nav/monthly-txt.html)). Netherlands, Denmark and Switzerland publish identity registers and exchange prices only; Swiss Fund Data, the real full-universe NAV platform, prohibits systematic extraction, so Swiss NAV stays limited to about 47 listed funds plus 67 CH ETFs unless SIX FI grants a licence ([Swiss Fund Data](https://www.swissfunddata.ch/sfdpub/sfd-eingang)). Sweden's FI file has no unit NAV; Pensionsmyndigheten Fondtorg and Nasdaq Nordic are UNVERIFIED NAV routes, and manager files (Swedbank Robur, SEB, Handelsbanken) are untested.

For GB, IE and LU no regulator publishes NAV history; GB NAV rests on UNOFFICIAL FT and FundsLibrary snapshots (whose local CSVs were absent this session), and the only official-adjacent routes are LSE RNS announcements for investment trusts and issuer ETF files such as the SSGA holdings workbook, which was fetched ([SSGA SPDR UCITS example](https://www.ssga.com/library-content/products/fund-data/etfs/emea/holdings-daily-emea-en-spyw-gy.xlsx)). LU NAV from LuxSE is a licensed data product and its front-end GraphQL endpoint is undocumented and commercially restricted ([LuxSE data products](https://www.bourse.lu/data-product-range)). US classes without a ticker (about 9.4k) have no public daily NAV anywhere; GLEIF returned zero matches for US fund ISINs, so mutual-fund ISIN/CUSIP stay near-empty and ETF CUSIP via the SEC 13(f) list is UNVERIFIED. Canada has only Scotia's file today and needs per-manager endpoint discovery in a browser; Mexico's BMV JSON needs its POST payload captured; both need a human step.

In Asia, mainland off-exchange funds (about 12.7k of 14,170) have NAV only on the unreachable CSRC e-disclosure host or UNOFFICIAL aggregators such as Tiantian (fund.eastmoney.com), which must be flagged UNOFFICIAL. Taiwan mutual-fund NAV depends on SITCA `nav.csv`, which timed out from this host and probably needs a Taiwan-reachable network; TDCC's Fundclear API is blocked by reCAPTCHA ([Fundclear](https://www.fundclear.com.tw/)). Singapore has no official NAV aggregator for unit trusts. Hong Kong's roughly 1,900 offshore UCITS have NAV, ISIN and fees only in per-fund key facts statements; HKEX covers ETPs and MPFA gives month-end MPF prices only. Japan's IMAJ library is the only exhaustive free NAV source and forbids scraping; MUFG's keyless API covers only its own 542 funds. Korea's public-fund NAV is in KOFIA DIS, whose service names were not found by guessing; KRX ETF NAV via FSC is KOGL type 4 (non-commercial).

For the rest, Russia has no official bulk NAV or net asset file (the Bank of Russia publishes quarterly aggregates only; MOEX ISS gives exchange closes, not NAV), so daily NAV needs per-management-company adapters, with Investfunds as a flagged UNOFFICIAL fallback. South Africa's NAV comes from manager sites (M&G offers a 12-month all-funds price download, UNVERIFIED format) or vendors; JSE data is paid and blocked. UAE has no central NAV platform; the practical fallback is manager PDFs and the DIB distributor list, classified as distributor-official, not regulator.

Twelve Data exposes 11 mutual-fund routes, with the world sections restricted to Ultra plans and ISIN/CUSIP separately paywalled ([Twelve Data MF docs](https://twelvedata.com/docs/mutual-funds/mf-all-data)). The fields classify as directly official, derivable or proprietary, and the table adds the best official route plus the best country coverage found in this research.

| risk/ratings, category ranks, ESG score, valuation metrics, brokerages | Proprietary | Not reproducible; EU SRI/SRRI and IBK risk grade kept as separate official fields |

DB additions build on the earlier schema proposal ([twelvedata_schema_and_global_ids.md](/media/asim/4_TB_1/MFAPI/MF%20ETL%20PIPELINE/research_notes/Global%20official%20fund%20data%20sources/twelvedata_schema_and_global_ids.md)) and this research adds specific drivers for each. A `fund_identifiers` table (scheme, value, MIC, source) is needed because FIRDS and HMRC attach ISINs per share class while the LU/IE rows are sub-funds, and because LEI, CSSF code, BaFin-ID, FINMA-ID, AMF `parId`, CBR rules number, USI and KOFIA short code all need homes. A `fund_share_classes` table is required for GECO shares, HMRC lines, Skat rows and BetaShares-type classes. A `fund_fees` table fits FI fee trees, JPX TER, CBR showcase fees, COVIP costs and CPF expense ratios. A `fund_distributions` table holds GECO coupons and ETF dividends. A `fund_benchmarks` table holds SSE `INDEX_NAME`, JPX underlying index, TWSE index, CBR benchmark, CVM `Indicador_Desempenho`. A `fund_risk_indicator` table must keep SRI, FI standard deviation, MUFG risk and KR 1-6 grades distinct. Three additions are new in this research: a `market_country` (distribution market) beside `domicile_country`, because HK and DE lists contain offshore funds; a `price_type` flag (`nav` versus `market_price`) so Euronext, SIX, MOEX and JPX prices do not enter NAV history; and a `source_tier` label (`official_regulator`, `official_exchange`, `official_issuer`, `distributor`, `unofficial`) plus a stored unit/currency for net assets (FI is SEK in whole krona per the median value but the unit is undocumented, GECO is thousand EUR). Mexico needs a corrected catalog (blank ISIN, `id_emision` as a secondary id), and Korea needs `currency` left null until the FSC master is joined, then an assumed-KRW flag if the project accepts the research recommendation.

## Phased roadmap: highest yield, verified and easy first

The ranking weights funds unlocked, ease and verified-ness. Phase 0 needs no new network access.

**Phase 0 (days, existing files or one-line fixes).**

## What the research did not cover

Several researchers stopped before their budget, and the following remain unverified rather than negative findings. Local snapshot CSVs for FT, FundsLibrary, Allianz LU, Euronext, Borsa Italiana and La Cote Bleue were absent from the checkout, so row counts could not be re-measured; no fuzzy-match rate to the DB was computed for any join (names versus ISIN lists), so all "expected fill" figures are estimates. Not tested: the SEC 13(f) list, N-PORT class returns, Canadian manager endpoints and CIFSC terms, Euronext product pages and ETF MICs for Milan, Consob and Banca d'Italia registers, Pensionsmyndigheten downloads, Nasdaq Nordic endpoints, B3 and Receita CNPJ layouts, the CDA, lâmina and perfil mensal headers, MAS OPERA record count, CPF PDF layouts, HKEX ETP data endpoint, KFS PDF layouts, the KOFIA NAV service, FSC roster size, EDINET form codes, JSE data products, DFSA/ADX/DFM ETF lists, CMA export terms, and most manager NAV pages (Japan, Netherlands, South Africa, Switzerland, UAE). GECO's NAV coverage rests on a 34-share sample, and the 75% rate is not a universe figure. The Korean currency default (KRW) is a research inference, not a verified field. Rate limits are undocumented for almost every source; the notes advise 1 request per second as a polite default. Counts for the Bank of Russia benchmark fill, and the legal effect of sanctions, are unconfirmed.

## Conclusion

The research changes the plan in three ways. First, the largest remaining gaps are not mainly missing sources but unused columns and uncrawled endpoints: GECO's per-share API and the cached compartment file lift France from 90 NAV rows and zero manager/inception fields, the FINMA XLSX and CSRC inception column are already on disk, and the Bank of Spain, CBI and BdE files each carry unparsed fields. Second, the honest ceiling is lower than Twelve Data parity: official free sources give identity, NAV for roughly a third of the universe, fees and holdings in a few markets, but no free official ratings, category ranks, ESG scores or fund-level fees/SRI in Europe outside KID PDFs. Third, licensing, not technology, now gates several good-looking sources (Swiss Fund Data, IMAJ, KRX, ISS), so the DB should carry a source tier and price-type flag from the start.

The sequencing that follows is: finish the Phase 0 mapping fixes, build the GECO crawler and the ESMA/HMRC/GLEIF identity stack, add Russia, then take the verified exchange and issuer feeds. In parallel, a person should spend an hour in a browser on the access-limited items in the verification table (CVM, CNBV, SITCA, JSE, IFIC, KOFIA, DFSA/ADX) and register the EDINET, data.go.kr and FCA keys, because each unblocked item converts an UNVERIFIED line in this report into a connector.
