# Official fund data: fix bugs first, then add India

Free official sources can make the pipeline's data resemble Twelve Data's mutual-fund data, but only unevenly, and several of the cheapest wins are bug fixes in code that already exists. Three kinds of gap dominate the DB. Holdings exist for the US only: 33,372 of 39,139 US funds have holdings, against zero in every other country. NAV is missing for most of CN, IE, ES, ZA, DK, SE and JP. ISIN, ticker, inception date and net assets are near-empty outside the US, BR and MX. The highest-value, best-evidenced additions are, in order: (a) four fixes to existing connectors (Hong Kong SFC parser, Brazil CDA holdings, Spain CNMV XML, Sweden FI holdings); (b) India's AMFI `NAVAll.txt`, which was fetched and verified as a free keyless daily file with scheme code, ISINs, category, AMC and NAV; (c) Thailand's SEC API (free key), Taiwan's SITCA CSV, and free ESMA/GLEIF identity data for Europe. For LU, FR, IE and DE no free official bulk NAV history exists; it is sold by LuxSE, Euronext, Kneip, SIX and OeKB, so the fallback is forward-building history from the daily snapshots the pipeline already ingests. Fields Twelve Data gets from a proprietary taxonomy (ratings, category ranks, ESG scores, valuation metrics, brokerages) cannot be reproduced officially and should be returned as unavailable. A large share of the world was only partially researched, and this report labels every unverified claim as such (section 8).

Evidence labels used below: **VERIFIED** means a researcher fetched or curl-tested it on 2026-11-17 (or read it in the repo). **UNVERIFIED** means it comes from search snippets or background knowledge and must be tested before coding. **UNOFFICIAL** means a third-party wrapper or vendor. The research notes gave only approximate fund counts for most sources, so counts are marked as estimates.

## Best official source per country: India, Thailand, Taiwan, Spain and Brazil are usable; Western Europe is not

### Where free official bulk data exists (or likely exists)

India is the clear first target. AMFI's `NAVAll.txt` was fetched: it is semicolon-delimited with the header `Scheme Code;ISIN Div Payout/ ISIN Growth;ISIN Div Reinvestment;Scheme Name;Plan;Option;Net Asset Value;Date`, dates are `DD-Mon-YYYY`, no key is needed, and category headings and AMC names appear as non-data lines that give category and manager (**VERIFIED**) ([AMFI NAVAll](https://portal.amfiindia.com/spages/NAVAll.txt)). The history route is weaker than it looks: AMFI's web page limits historical downloads to 90 days per request ([AMFI NAV history](https://www.amfiindia.com/net-asset-value/nav-history)), and the `DownloadNAVHistoryReport_Po.aspx` URL pattern comes from third-party tools, may have moved in the AMFI site redesign, and must be HTTP-tested (**UNVERIFIED**) ([freefincal tool](https://freefincal.com/excel-mutual-fund-nav-history-downloader/)). Fund count is an estimate of 40-45k rows including plan/option variants (not counted). AMFI publishes AUM reports and scheme details ([AMFI other data](https://www.amfiindia.com/otherdata)), but no official bulk file for TER, riskometer, benchmark or holdings was found: SEBI requires monthly AMC portfolio files, but they are per-AMC Excel/PDF on about 45 AMC sites (scraping only). AMFI states no explicit open licence and disclaims accuracy, so commercial redistribution terms are unknown.

Thailand's SEC OpenAPI V2 (api.sec.or.th) documents factsheet fees, performance, statistics, asset allocation, top-5 holdings and daily NAV endpoints with a free developer key, per a community Go client that documents the official API ([sec-go](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/jwitmann/sec-go)). The official portal returned 403 to the researcher's fetch, so endpoint docs, quota, history depth and licence are all unconfirmed (**UNVERIFIED**) ([SEC Open Data](https://secopendata.sec.or.th/Fund/MF_IPO)).

Taiwan's SITCA open CSV is licensed for reuse (Open Government Data License 1.0), updated daily, and carries date, member ID, company name, fund code, fund name, NAV, change, type, currency and beneficiary-certificate code, but only for domestic funds ([data.gov.tw 11109](https://data.gov.tw/en/datasets/11109)). The file URL is `https://www.sitca.org.tw/MemberK0000/F/03/nav.csv`; curl from the research host timed out at 25 seconds with 0 bytes (**VERIFIED** timeout, cause unknown), and no connector exists. A companion SITCA dataset has ISIN, share class and fund manager but was last updated in May 2021 ([NCHC mirror](https://scidm.nchc.org.tw/en/dataset/b1e1f0ab-a78b-4609-aee7-9741cd319ea3/resource/5cfd2ca5-1cae-4d95-bb26-58c50ab27f8f)). History depth of nav.csv appears to be current day only (inference), so Taiwan also needs forward-building.

Spain's CNMV is the best "already downloaded, only partly used" source. The monthly files carry NAV, net assets and unitholder counts, and the quarterly files carry fees, returns and portfolio composition, as XML; download links are opaque tokens, so the index page must be scraped each period, and no reuse licence text was read ([CNMV downloads](https://www.cnmv.es/portal/Publicaciones/descarga-informacion-individual?ejercicio=2026)). The repo catalog says the NAV file has no currency field. Brazil's CVM open data (ODbL) is the right source for registry, daily NAV, monthly portfolios (CDA) and the lâmina (fees, risk), keyed on CNPJ ([CVM CDA](https://dados.cvm.gov.br/dataset/fi-doc-cda), [CVM lâmina](https://dados.cvm.gov.br/dataset/fi-doc-lamina)). Hungary's BAMOSZ (an association, semi-official) offers a download tool where the user enters ISINs and fields and gets a CSV ([BAMOSZ](https://bamosz.hu/en/letoltesek)); history depth, fund count and terms are unconfirmed.

Argentina, Chile and Colombia are credible but largely unverified. CAFCI (the industry chamber that is the official FCI price publisher) has an undocumented JSON backend that community tools use; the specific URLs `api.cafci.org.ar/fondo/{id}/clase/{id}/ficha` and the fund list pattern are from background knowledge (**UNVERIFIED**), and terms are unread ([fedemoglia/cafci-api, UNOFFICIAL](https://github.com/fedemoglia/cafci-api)). Chile's CMF says daily cuota values per fund and series, and a daily cartola per fund, are on its site ([CMF Educa](https://www.cmfchile.cl/educa/621/w3-propertyvalue-504.html)), but cmfchile.cl did not resolve from the research host. Colombia's SFC publishes daily F-523 profitability reports from 1 January 2016 through a web generator, under an open-data licence (Resolution 1519 of 2020), but download format and fields are unconfirmed ([SFC FIC report](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/ReporteInformacionFIC/), [SFC licence](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/10116019)).

Smaller or lower-confidence candidates, all needing probing, are listed together here. Pakistan's MUFAP publishes NAV and returns tables with Excel export for roughly 250-300 funds (estimate), with no documented API ([MUFAP](https://www.mufap.com.pk/nav_returns_performance.php?tab=05&)). Malaysia's FIMM shows daily unit prices by management company in HTML with no confirmed download ([FIMM](https://www.fimm.com.my/report/dailyprice.php)). Nigeria's SEC posts weekly and monthly CIS NAV pages per year (file format not inspected) ([SEC Nigeria](https://sec.gov.ng/for-operators/keep-track-of-capital-market-data/net-asset-value-data/weekly-net-asset-value-for-cis/2025-weekly-nav-for-cis/)). Saudi Exchange's fund screener shows NAV per unit, manager, currency, Shari'ah flag, expense ratio and inception, but is a dynamic page with some cached pages dated 2024-25 ([Saudi Exchange screener](https://www.tadawulgroup.sa/wps/portal/saudiexchange/newsandreports/fund-screener?locale=en)). Hong Kong's MPFA publishes monthly MPF unit-price XLS files, 12 months at a time; a test request for the October 2026 file returned HTTP 200 (**VERIFIED**), but columns and terms are unstated ([MPFA](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/info-centre/fund-information/monthly-fund-price/monthly-unit-prices-of-mpf-constituent-funds)). Turkey's TEFAS has a working per-fund JSON API per a third-party crawler (price only, look-back up to 5 years, one request per fund) but the site returned a WAF rejection to the researcher and the API is not officially documented ([tefas-crawler, UNOFFICIAL](https://github.com/burakyilmaz321/tefas-crawler)). Japan's EDINET API v2 returned HTTP 401 without a key (**VERIFIED**), so a free key is required ([EDINET spec](https://disclosure2dl.edinet-fsa.go.jp/guide/static/disclosure/download/ESE140206.pdf)); fund filings carry NAV, holdings, manager and fees but at one document per fund per period, so only coarse history.

## What Twelve Data exposes, what is official, and the schema additions needed

### The Twelve Data surface and its gating

Twelve Data's mutual-fund API has a 1-credit directory (over 140,000 funds on Ultra and above, 50 records on lower plans) and eight "world" routes (full data at 1,000 credits, and summary, performance, risk, ratings, composition, purchase_info and sustainability at 200 credits each), all restricted to Ultra and above ([MF directory](https://twelvedata.com/docs/mutual-funds/mutual-funds-list), [MF full data](https://twelvedata.com/docs/mutual-funds/mf-all-data)). ISIN, CUSIP and FIGI are separately paywalled add-ons, so identifiers are exactly where official sources add value. Twelve Data's NAV time series is a daily OHLC where open, high, low and close are all the NAV, per the project's own 2026-11-13 comparison ([repo report](reports/twelvedata_us_fund_comparison_2026-11-13.md)). The project's own gap assessment records that Twelve Data's terms bar redistribution and competing use, so its values must not be ingested; FundFlow data must come from official sources or from FundFlow's own calculations ([repo gap doc](docs/TWELVE_DATA_MUTUAL_FUNDS_GAP_ASSESSMENT.md)). The `/funds`, `/etf/world`, `/quote`, `/profile` and `/time_series` fund-specific field lists could not be fetched (404 or undocumented), so the map below covers only the documented mutual-fund routes.

### Field map: directly official, computable, or proprietary

This classification is the researchers' analysis, not a vendor statement. D = directly published by an official source; C = computable from official NAV, distributions or holdings; P = proprietary.

| Twelve Data field | Class | Official route or note |
|---|---|---|
| name, country, currency, fund_family | D | Registers, SEC series/class, KID, AMFI AMC, CVM administrator |
| symbol (vendor id such as `0P0001LCQ3`) | P | Replace with ISIN, ticker and national id |
| exchange, mic_code | D where listed | ESMA FIRDS, exchange lists; most mutual funds have none |
| fund_type (e.g. "Small Blend") | P | Official equivalents are coarse (CFI, CVM class, AMFI category, SEBI category); store the source category, do not relabel |
| performance, risk, return rating; category_return; rank_in_category; all `*_category` metrics | P | Not reproducible; EU SRI/SRRI is a different 1-7 scale and must be a separate field |
| share_class_inception_date | D | SEC class, KID, registers; CVM registry |
| nav, net_assets, min_investment, expense ratios, turnover, overview text, people/tenure | D | Prospectus, KID, N-PORT, N-MFP, registers, AMFI AUM, issuer pages |
| 12-month low/high, ytd_return, trailing, annual, quarterly returns, yield | C | From stored NAV history plus distributions (label price vs total return) |
| alpha, beta, r_squared, std, Sharpe, Treynor, max drawdown | C | Needs NAV series plus a benchmark series and risk-free rate; benchmark index data may be licensed |
| top_holdings, asset_allocation | C from D holdings | Needs an ISIN-to-ticker map (OpenFIGI) |
| major_market_sectors | C, with licensed taxonomies (GICS/ICB) the obstacle | Use CFI or issuer classification as a free approximation |
| bond_breakdown (maturity, duration, credit quality) | C partial | N-PORT gives maturity and some fields; credit grade often absent |
| valuation_metrics (P/E, P/B, median market cap) | C, but needs commercial fundamentals | Not realistically official |
| sustainability score and pillars, corporate_aum, brokerages | P | EU SFDR article and EET flags are official but not a numeric score |

Several mapping facts are verified: N-PORT is quarterly series-level holdings and not a daily NAV source, N-MFP covers money-market holdings and class NAV, and raw filings do not give comparable 0-5 ratings, per the repo gap document. FIRDS provides ISIN, MIC and CFI weekly (full) and daily (delta) for MiFID-traded instruments, which covers ETFs and listed funds but not unlisted mutual funds ([ESMA FIRDS instructions](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-12/ESMA65-8-5240_FIRDS_Transparency_Download_Instructions.pdf)). GLEIF's daily ISIN-LEI files are CC0, but currently cover only new ISINs from early-mover numbering agencies, so absence does not mean no LEI ([GLEIF](https://www.gleif.org/en/lei-data/lei-mapping/download-isin-to-lei-relationship-files)). OpenFIGI's free mapping API allows 25 requests a minute unkeyed and 250 keyed, with 10 and 100 jobs per request ([OpenFIGI docs](https://www.openfigi.com/api/documentation)); mutual-fund coverage is unverified. EU EMT and EPT templates are B2B Excel/XML exchanges, not public bulk, and commercial feeds such as Kneip and fefundinfo redistribute them ([BVI EPT](https://bvi.de/en/services/samples-and-working-aids/european-priips-template-ept-comfort-european-priips-template-cept)).

### Proposed canonical field set and DB additions

The existing schema (`app/models/schema.py`) has `funds` (isin, legal_name, provider, category, fund_type, inception_date, status, ticker, manager, administrator, net_assets and date, sec_series_id, sec_cik, holdings_status, holdings_report_date), `nav_observations`, `portfolio_holdings` (isin, cusip, ticker, market_value, weight), `holding_metrics` and `fund_profile_snapshots` (JSONB, keyed by dataset). It lacks LEI, FIGI, share-class structure, typed fees, distributions, benchmark and a metrics cache. The canonical set should reuse Twelve Data's section names as an interface but tag every value with `source`, `as_of` and `basis` (reported or computed): identity (including ISIN, ticker plus MIC, LEI, FIGI, national id, manager, administrator, source-reported category and CFI, a normalised FundFlow asset class, distribution policy, inception, status), price (NAV and date), cost, size, computed returns, computed risk statistics plus the official SRI/SRRI as its own field, composition with coverage percentage, and document links. Proprietary sections are returned as unavailable. The minimal additions proposed, in priority order, are: `fund_identifiers` (scheme, value, MIC, source, validity), `fund_share_classes` (twelve Data is class-level and the `funds` table mixes class and series), `fund_fees` (typed, filterable), `fund_distributions` (needed for total return and yield), `fund_benchmarks` plus benchmark series, `fund_risk_indicator`, `fund_metrics_cache` (with method version and an inputs-complete flag), `fund_allocation` (reported or computed buckets), extra `portfolio_holdings` columns (figi, issuer lei, asset category, issuer country, maturity) with a `security_master`, `fund_people`, and `fund_documents`, with provenance columns (`source_id`, `source_url`, `as_of`, `retrieved_at`, `license_status`) everywhere. The research did not check whether existing Alembic migrations already hold some of these.

## Four bugs and quick wins already visible in the pipeline

**Hong Kong SFC parser (about 2,255 funds extracted, 0 loaded).** The report `reports/latest_run.md` records 2,255 extracted and 0 loaded, and `reports/hong_kong_rejected_records.jsonl` is full of "duplicate CE number with conflicting names". The cause was confirmed in `app/connectors/hk_sfc_funds.py` (`_load`): `re.search(r"\(([A-Za-z0-9]+)\)", target)` returns the first parenthetical, but the SFC cells look like `Name (USD) (BYK033)`, so the CE number becomes `USD`, `HK` or `LUX` and hundreds of distinct funds collide. The fix is to take the last group matching `\(([A-Z]{3}\d{3})\)\s*$` and strip only that suffix; each sub-fund also appears twice in the HTML, so dedupe by CE ref. The page also has authorisation date and a derivative-fund flag that are not parsed. HK rows have null domicile, so the country must be set (as for KR) or they will land in the "??" bucket. The page is [the SFC product list](https://apps.sfc.hk/productlistWeb/searchProduct/UTMF.do?lang=EN) (**VERIFIED**).

**Brazil holdings are zero for 37,875 funds, though CDA is in the catalog.** The catalog text says CDA holdings are imported via `sync-cvm-holdings`, yet the DB has none. Three CVM changes are the candidate causes (an inference; the researchers could not open CVM pages because dados.cvm.gov.br returned HTTP 403 Cloudflare to their host, so file names and headers are unverified): a notice dated 26 January 2026 says `TP_FUNDO` became `TP_FUNDO_CLASSE` and `CNPJ_FUNDO` became `CNPJ_FUNDO_CLASSE` in all files; notices from 15 November 2025 rename CDA files to `cda_fie_AAAAMM.csv` and `cda_fie_CONFID_AAAAMM.csv`, with index funds in `cda_fiim_AAAAMM.csv`; and the registry moved to `registro_fundo_classe.zip` (registro_fundo, registro_classe, registro_subclasse), with conflicting notices about which URL is current ([CVM notices](https://dados.cvm.gov.br/pages/novidades), [CVM CDA](https://dados.cvm.gov.br/dataset/fi-doc-cda)). A connector keyed on the old column or file names fails silently. The legacy `cad_fi.csv`, which carried inception date and fee fields, is UNVERIFIED background knowledge; the new registry's field names are also unconfirmed. Fund-level ISIN is not a CVM registry field; ISIN exists only on CDA holding rows (`CD_ISIN`, UNVERIFIED name), so BR fund ISIN stays largely unfillable.

**Sweden FI XML holdings are not imported.** Per the repo catalog, the FI quarterly ZIP/XML has names, FI fund numbers, ISINs, holdings and portfolio values but no unit NAV, and only identity is imported, which explains SE's 688 ISINs and zero holdings. Importing the holdings is a small extension of an existing download.

**CNMV XML carries NAV, fees and portfolio but only the register is loaded.** ES has 3,116 funds with ISIN and currency only. The monthly XML provides NAV, net assets and unitholders; the quarterly XML provides fees, returns and portfolio composition; this is the largest single fill of an existing country (the research ranks it first by value). Related quick wins are the Korean IBK CSV (1,132 rows with null domicile and currency, which is why they sit in the "??" bucket, so set them from source market), FIRDS filtered to CFI starting with "C" to give ISIN, currency and issuer LEI for DE, IE and GB (partial coverage, listed instruments only), and scheduling every snapshot connector to persist its daily output.

## Licensing and terms risks that can block production use

Terms were read for very few sources, so the safest default is that an absent licence statement is not a permission. The specific risks found are as follows. **Swiss Fund Data** is the FINMA-recognised publication platform and its terms prohibit systematic extraction or scraping by data distributors; SIX became sole owner in January 2025 ([SFD](https://www.swissfundplatform.ch/en/swiss-fund-data), [SIX release](https://www.six-group.com/en/newsroom/media-releases/2025/20250117-six-sfd.html)). The existing `ch_six_fund_explorer` connector reads a SIX file (`ref.json`) and should be checked against the same terms. **IMAJ** (Japan) search library terms prohibit robots, so the connector is registered but deliberately not scraped; it is the only free daily-NAV bulk-like source for Japan and should be used manually or via a licence. **KOGL type 4** (attribution, no commercial use, no modification) applies to the Korean FSC/KRX datasets found, such as the FSC fund-sales API ([data.go.kr](https://www.data.go.kr/data/15151230/openapi.do)); the KRX trust-security prices are also flagged no-redistribution in the repo. KOFIA OpenAPI requires application and approval, and KRX ETF data restricts redistribution (UNVERIFIED). **TEFAS** is accessed only through an unofficial API behind a WAF, with terms unread. **CVM** data is ODbL, which has share-alike and attribution obligations. **Taiwan SITCA** is under Open Government Data License 1.0. **GLEIF** data is CC0. **OpenFIGI** FIGI identifiers are presented as unrestricted, but mapping from third-party IDs may carry the other provider's licence; the terms text was not read in full ([OpenFIGI terms](https://openfigi.com/docs/terms-of-service)). **ISINs** are not necessarily free to redistribute, and ANNA's free lookup limits downloads ([ANNA FAQ](https://anna-web.org/faq-page/)); Euronext ETF Master File, Kneip, SIX PRIIPs data, OeKB FundsXML and Oslo Børs feeds are paid. **AMFI** disclaims accuracy and publishes no open licence. **MPFA** does not guarantee accuracy of trustee prices. **Twelve Data** itself must not be used as a data source (repo gap document). **Russia** sources raise sanctions and access questions for a Western pipeline. Many sources (CNMV, BAMOSZ, MUFAP, FIMM, Saudi Exchange, CAFCI) had no terms or robots check at all.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that the cheapest path to Twelve-Data-like coverage is not new data but unblocking data already in reach: one regex in the HK connector, CVM file renames, the CNMV XML and the Swedish holdings XML are worth far more funds per hour than any new country. Beyond that, India stands alone as a verified, free, keyless bulk source with NAV and ISIN together, while the large European domiciles (LU, FR, IE, DE) have no free official NAV at all, so history there will only exist if the pipeline starts accumulating its own snapshots now.

The practical implication is that "similar to Twelve Data" will mean an honest hybrid: official identity, NAV, size and fees where published, FundFlow-computed returns and risk labelled as computed, and explicit unavailability for ratings, category ranks, ESG scores and valuation metrics. Because many of the most attractive sources (CVM, TEFAS, Thailand SEC, SITCA, EDINET, CAFCI) were blocked, keyed or timed out during research, the first deliverable should be a one-day HTTP verification pass from an unblocked network, which will convert most of the UNVERIFIED roadmap entries into either committed work or crossed-out leads.
