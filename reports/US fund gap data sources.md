# FundFlow can close most US gaps, but not CUSIP, category or NAV, for free

FundFlow can close its profile and holdings gaps from SEC data, but ISIN/CUSIP, Morningstar-style category and a deep daily NAV history need paid, contractually licensed sources. On the profile side, SEC risk/return (RR) data sets, N-CEN, N-PORT and tailored shareholder reports (TSR) supply inception dates, fees, objectives, benchmarks and holdings, and EDGAR content is free to reuse commercially. No source in the research gives a legitimate, free route to share-class CUSIPs or ISINs: CUSIP Global Services (CGS) restricts redistribution, and the only open alternative is FIGI. No regulator publishes daily NAV; the institutional origin is the Nasdaq Fund Network (NFN), so a defensible NAV history means licensing NFN directly or through a reseller. Matching Twelve Data (TD) is achievable only for public facts: identity, issuer-published NAV on a stated date, inception date, prospectus fees and SEC-filed holdings. It is not achievable for category labels, ratings, risk statistics or ESG scores, which are vendor-licensed. Copying TD values into FundFlow would breach TD's terms as relayed in the research. Two caveats apply throughout. Third-party terms of service below came through summarizing fetch tools and are unverified against the live pages. This is research, not legal advice.

## Where FundFlow stands: the gaps were near-total

The project owner measured **39,139 US share classes** before this work: 38,970 lacked an ISIN, 38,044 lacked a category, 38,747 lacked an inception date, 9,596 lacked a ticker, 13,445 had no NAV and 5,767 had no holdings. Most funds with NAV had only 6-30 daily points, Yahoo-sourced and starting 2026-10-02. SEC risk/return data sets have since filled inception dates for 16,838 classes (43.0%), tickers for 31,688 (81.0%), and net expense ratios, turnover, objective text and 1/5/10-year returns for tens of thousands of classes; tailored shareholder reports added another 1,562 tickers, 2,382 inception dates and class-level sector weights. ISIN coverage rose to 770 classes from validated N-PORT CUSIP/ISIN pairs. That is a real gain, but 43.0% inception coverage and 2.0% ISIN coverage show the limit of official sources. Two further structural facts matter. The Yahoo-sourced NAV is not licensable for a commercial API: the yfinance README says the tool is for personal, research and educational use, and its Apache licence covers only the code, not the data ([yfinance README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ranaroussi/yfinance@main/README.md)). Second-hand quotes of Yahoo's developer terms bar deriving income from its APIs without written permission ([Yahoo API terms](https://legal.yahoo.com/us/en/yahoo/terms/product-atos/apitnc/index.html)). That is the largest legal exposure in the current data and should be replaced, not extended.

The market gap is also clear. TD gates its rich mutual-fund analytics (summary, performance, risk, ratings, composition, purchase info, sustainability) to the Ultra tier at about $999 a month and 200 credits per call, and its docs name no upstream source ([TD docs](https://twelvedata.com/docs/mutual-funds/mf-summary), [TD pricing](https://twelvedata.com/pricing)). TD's pricing page is internally inconsistent, with structured data showing $29/$99/$329 against displayed $79/$229/$999, so a price comparison should be re-checked live. TD's ISIN and CUSIP are paid add-ons, with FIGI on higher plans ([TD llms-full](https://twelvedata.com/llms-full.txt)).

## Profile fields: SEC data fills most of them, labelled as filed

The SEC RR data sets are the best official bulk source, keyed by series (S...) and class (C...) IDs. They carry class fees, objective and strategy text, benchmark performance and inception dates (`AverageAnnualReturnInceptionDate`; `oef:PerfInceptionDate` in the newer taxonomy). They have been produced monthly since late 2026, consolidated into quarterly and annual files, and a new SCN file adds series/class IDs, names and tickers ([SEC RR page](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/mutual-fund-prospectus-riskreturn-summary-data-sets), [RR readme](https://SEC.gov/dera/data/rr1.pdf)). The researcher saw no 2024 or 2017 file on the page, so 2024-filed facts may need direct EDGAR XBRL or later sets. The SEC states the data are "as filed" and may contain errors. Coverage is limited to funds that file XBRL risk/return exhibits, and the exact coverage fraction was never found. The inception-date coverage of 43.0% is the owner's measurement of that limit. Taking the latest filing per series and class, and adding the SCN file, are the first moves.

Tailored shareholder reports add fresher class data. Since the July 2024 compliance date they must show net assets, holdings count, turnover and 1/5/10-year returns in Inline XBRL ([SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2022/10/s7-09-20), [SEC ADI 2024-14](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-investment-management/accounting-disclosure-information/adi-2024-14-tailored-shareholder-report-common-issues)). The open-source EdgarTools library parses per-class ticker, expense ratio and inception date ([EdgarTools](https://edgartools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guides/fundshareholderreport-data-object-guide/)). The researcher found no SEC bulk data set for TSR, so extraction is per-filing. N-CEN quarterly data sets supply type flags (ETF, index, target date, fund of funds) ([SEC N-CEN](https://www.sec.gov/dera/data/form-ncen-data-sets)).

Two profile fields have no clean official tag. A grep of the RR and OEF taxonomy guides found no minimum-initial-investment tag and no portfolio-manager tag, so those fields live in prospectus prose, if anywhere ([RR guide](https://xbrl.sec.gov/rr/2023/rr-preparers-guide-2022-11-04.pdf), [OEF guide](https://xbrl.sec.gov/oef/2025/oeftaxonomyguide-2025-03-17.pdf)). Class-level net assets are also a gap, since TSR net assets appeared report-level in the parser.

On reuse, the SEC says its public content may be copied and redistributed without permission, with attribution and no use of its seal or "SEC"/EDGAR in business names ([SEC privacy page](https://www.sec.gov/about/privacy-information)). Automated access is capped at 10 requests per second with an identifying User-Agent ([SEC fair access](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)). A statutory summary says EDGAR information may be resold without added fees (secondary source, [15 USC 78ll](https://m.openjurist.org/book/export/html/13385567)). Commercial reuse of SEC-sourced fields is therefore low risk.

## Category cannot be matched; a derived taxonomy is the legitimate substitute

The research found no official or open equivalent to Morningstar or Lipper categories. The SEC offers only coarse prevalent-asset-class and flag groupings ([SEC statistics](https://www.sec.gov/files/investment/im-investment-registered-fund-statistics-20250814.pdf)). TD's `fund_type` values, such as "Small Blend" in its documentation sample, and its `0P...` symbol format resemble Morningstar naming and identifiers, but that is inference; TD's docs say ratings are calculated "in-house ... and from various financial institutions" and name no source. Either way, TD category labels are vendor data that FundFlow cannot reproduce from regulator filings, and a match would be coincidence.

FundFlow can build its own labelled-as-derived taxonomy from N-CEN flags, N-PORT asset mix, the benchmark index name in RR/TSR, and objective-text classification. That yields coarse buckets (equity, bond, allocation, money market, US versus international, index versus active, target-date year, fund of funds). Style box, sector and duration/credit buckets would need holdings-to-fundamentals joins. FundFlow should not name these buckets after Morningstar or Lipper categories. Licensed routes exist. Morningstar's Essentials toolkit includes a distribution licence covering ratings, categories and style box, with marketing review and prescribed disclosure language ([Morningstar Essentials guidelines](https://advisor.morningstar.com/Enterprise/VTC/Essentials_Guidelines_US_2023_0323.pdf)). LSEG Lipper requires a Fund Marketing License to publish rankings and ratings ([LSEG Lipper](https://www.lseg.com/en/data-analytics/asset-management-solutions/lipper-fund-performance)). Both are quote-only, and no price was found. Scraping Morningstar, Yahoo or Lipper categories into a commercial dataset is likely a terms violation, though it was not verified site by site.

## ISIN and CUSIP: licensing, not data availability, is the barrier

The SEC `company_tickers_mf.json` file gives registrant ID, series ID, class ID and ticker, but no CUSIP or ISIN ([SEC file](https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers_mf.json)). A US ISIN is "US" plus the 9-character CUSIP plus a check digit, assigned by CGS as the US numbering agency ([CUSIP.com](https://www.cusip.com/identifiers.html), [ANNA FAQ](https://anna-web.org/faq-page/)). So publishing ISINs means publishing CUSIPs. CGS's website terms limit CUSIP database use to internal trading and settlement and bar publishing or distributing the database or subsets ([CUSIP.com](https://www.cusip.com/identifiers.html)). Commentary on CGS contracts says they require firms to guarantee they will not cut the CUSIP out of the ISIN or resell the ISIN ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/sps-purcell-discusses-the-european-commissions-ruling-regarding-us-isin-pricing-practices)).

**Source discrepancy, flagged.** The ISIN researcher read the SEC 13F securities-list page and reported no stated CUSIP usage restriction ([SEC 13F list](https://www.sec.gov/divisions/investment/13flists.htm)). The project owner's session read the 13F list's cover page directly. It states that CUSIP numbers are used with permission of CGS and carry "No redistribution without permission of CGS". This report treats the direct reading as authoritative. The practical consequence is that free SEC files containing CUSIPs, namely the 13F list and N-PORT holdings records, are not a safe basis for redistributing CUSIPs, even though the SEC data itself is public. The 13F list also would not cover open-end mutual funds, which are not 13(f) securities (researcher inference), so it is a partial source even for ETFs.

Fund-side commercial CUSIP sources are all paid. DTCC Fund/SERV's security master is a participant service ([DTCC MFPS II fact sheet](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/Investment-Product-Services/Wealth-Management-Services/Funds/MFPS-II-Fact-Sheet.pdf)), and Nasdaq's NFN formats carry CUSIP fields ([Nasdaq MFQS FAQ, 2011](https://nasdaqtrader.com/content/newsalerts/2011/dn/mfqs0050_faqs.pdf)). Xignite states its US NAV data is NFN-sourced and keyed by ticker, CIK, CUSIP and ISIN ([Xignite](https://www.xignite.com/blog/access-any-fund-nav-from-a-single-xignite-api)). No published redistributor price exists. Trade press cites end-user fees up to about $477,750 to $518,138 a year at the top tier, with the fee waived for users under 500 identifiers (figures unverified at source, paywalled) ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/regulation/7936086/class-action-lawsuit-takes-aim-at-cusip-sp-factset-aba)). The legal basis of CGS's claims is contested in a class action in which a Sherman Act Section 2 claim survived dismissal in July 2023 ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/node/7951120)). No final ruling was found, and the case gives FundFlow no safe harbour today.

The open alternative is FIGI. OpenFIGI's terms (2018, possibly stale) allow use, display, distribution and derivative works of FIGI identifiers, including passing them to customers ([OpenFIGI terms](https://openfigi.com/docs/terms-of-service)). The researcher did not confirm that OpenFIGI returns CUSIP or ISIN, or that share-class FIGI coverage of US mutual funds is complete. The sensible key design is SEC class ID plus ticker plus FIGI, with CUSIP/ISIN offered only once licensed, either from CGS/FactSet directly or via a vendor whose agreement allows onward redistribution.

## NAV history: the licensed feed is the only complete answer

No regulator publishes daily NAV. EDGAR yields periodic anchors only (financial highlights in N-CSR/N-CSRS, monthly net assets in N-PORT), which can validate a series but not supply it (researcher inference; form text was not retrieved). The institutional origin is NFN, formerly MFQS, which requires a Nasdaq distributor agreement. Its data policy has sections on delayed, end-of-day and historical data, identifier restrictions and licence restrictions, but the text was unreadable to the researcher ([NFN policy](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/AdministrationSupport/Policy/nasdaqfundnetworkpolicy.pdf), saved locally as a compressed PDF that can be extracted with pdftotext). A snippet says each feed recipient must be separately approved. Resellers include Xignite (history to 1994, per its blog) and dxFeed ([dxFeed NFN](https://www.dxfeed.com/consolidated-data-feed/nasdaq-mfds)). ICE offers fund reference and pricing data ([ICE](https://developer.ice.com/fixed-income-data-services/catalog/ice-reference-data-funds)). Intrinio names Cannon Valley Research as its source, offers about 12 years of history with full history as a separate charge, and is enterprise-only ([Intrinio](https://intrinio.com/products/mutual-fund-data)). None publishes a price.

Direct issuer sites are the weaker alternative. Vanguard's US terms (2018 PDF) bar devices providing repeated automated access without written authorization ([Vanguard US T&C](https://investor.vanguard.com/ts/pdf/terms_and_conditions.pdf)). Other families were not checked, and that question is largely unanswered. Aggregators are unsuitable as a source unless their enterprise redistribution licence covers mutual funds in writing. Tiingo's internal-commercial tier forbids displaying or sharing the data with another party ([Tiingo pricing](https://tiingo.com/about/pricing)). FMP requires a specific agreement for public display ([FMP terms](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/terms-of-service)). TD's own terms are covered below. The Tiingo and EODHD sourcing for funds is undisclosed, and the researcher inferred NFN-type feeds without evidence.

## Holdings: SEC N-PORT and daily ETF files, with a structural lag

Holdings are the best-served gap. Under the regime in force, N-PORT reports are due within 60 days of quarter end and only the third-month report is public ([SEC interim final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2019/02/amendments-timing-requirements-filing-reports-form-n-port)). The 2024 amendments for monthly public release were deferred to Nov 2027 / May 2028 ([Thompson Hine](https://www.thompsonhine.com/insights/sec-extends-compliance-date-for-amendments-to-form-n-port)). A February 2026 proposal would keep public release quarterly; no final adoption was found as of May 2026 ([Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2026/05/11/sec-proposes-amendments-to-form-n-port-and-extends-compliance-dates-for-names-rule-reporting-(registered-funds-regulatory-update))). The SEC publishes quarterly N-PORT data sets (2019 Q4 to 2026 Q3), covering management companies and ETFs organized as unit investment trusts, but excluding money market funds ([SEC N-PORT data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets)). Holdings in these files are as of the quarter end, up to about two months old when released, and a new series will not appear until its first report is filed and the next quarterly zip is posted. This is the likely reason 5,767 classes lacked holdings. Besides new funds, researcher inferences include non-calendar fiscal quarters, liquidations and mergers, series-versus-class mapping failures, money market funds (which file N-MFP, public 60 days after month end, [SEC N-MFP readme](https://sec.gov/files/nmfp_readme.pdf)), and 1933 Act commodity trusts such as GLD, which file 10-Ks ([GLD 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1222333/000119312515387033/d62404d10k.htm)).

For ETFs, Rule 6c-11 requires daily publication of full holdings (ticker, CUSIP or other identifier, description, quantity, weight) on a free public website before the open ([17 CFR 270.6c-11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/270.6c-11)). That gives official daily holdings for nearly every ETF, but in issuer-specific formats, and SEC staff noted some ETFs omitted identifiers ([SEC ADI 2025-15](https://sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-investment-management/accounting-disclosure-information/adi-2025-15-website-posting-requirements)). Reuse terms vary. iShares's report tool says downloads are "for your internal use only" ([iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/resources/tools/report-generation-tool/create-report)), and terms for SPDR, Invesco, Vanguard, ProShares and others were not retrieved. SEC-sourced holdings can be redistributed with low risk. Issuer-site holdings carry contractual risk and should be cleared per issuer. Note also that holdings records carrying CUSIPs inherit the CGS question above.

## What can match Twelve Data, field by field

The table sorts fields by what can legitimately match. TD's per-field sources, NAV date convention and rounding are not stated in its public docs, so "matches" means matches a correctly sourced independent value, not TD by construction.

| Field | Can match TD? | Legitimate source | Licensing and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ticker, class identity | Yes, with care | SEC class ID and ticker file | Open. Reuse after mergers causes mismatches. TD keys by vendor ID. |
| Inception date | Yes | RR/OEF filings, tailored shareholder reports | Open, as filed, 43.0% coverage so far. |
| Expense ratio (net/gross) | Yes, within fee-table date | RR (prospectus), TSR (actual) | Open. TD's as-of date unknown. Decimals versus percent differ. |
| Objective, benchmark | Approximately | RR text blocks | Open. Text will not match verbatim. |
| Holdings | Partly | N-PORT; ETF daily files | SEC open. As-of dates differ from TD's. CUSIPs inside holdings carry the CGS issue. |
| Net assets | Only with matching as-of date | TSR, N-PORT | Fund versus class level and vendor dates differ. |
| NAV daily | Yes, given the same valuation date | NFN or licensed reseller | Paid licence. Date labelling, rounding and adjustment cause mismatches. |
| ISIN, CUSIP | Yes, if licensed | CGS or licensed vendor | Paid. Redistribution restricted. Both vendors use CGS. |
| Category, ratings, risk stats, ESG, category comparisons | No | Morningstar or Lipper licence | Cannot be derived from SEC data. Own derived taxonomy will differ. |
| Managers, minimum investment | Partly | Prospectus prose | No tags. Manual or NLP extraction. |

For NAV, the researchers documented several causes of apparent mismatch without confirming which TD uses: valuation-date versus publish-date labels, delayed or weekly NAVs producing repeated values ([Portfolio Performance forum](https://forum.portfolio-performance.info/t/daily-change-in-price-for-funds-with-delayed-or-weekly-nav-reporting/39372)), previous-day prices entered as current ([GnuCash list](https://lists.gnucash.org/pipermail/gnucash-user/2020-June/091624.html)), and TD's time-series `adjust` default of `splits` (dividend-adjusted requires `all`) and variable `dp` ([TD time series](https://twelvedata.com/docs/market-data/time-series)). FundFlow should publish its date convention, adjustment policy and rounding explicitly.

## Twelve Data's terms bar using it as a source or a benchmark input

As relayed by the summarizing tool and unverified against the live page ([TD terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms)), TD allows data only for Internal Use (s.2.2(a)), bars redistribution, resale and sublicensing (s.2.3(b), s.17.1(f)), restricts caching (s.2.3(g)), bars building competitive products (s.2.3(d)), and bars combining its data with other sources to create competing products without permission (s.2.3(k)). Ingesting TD values to populate or "fix" FundFlow's data is therefore clearly outside those terms, and a competitor using TD data to benchmark its own product sits uncomfortably close to s.2.3(d) and (k). Benchmarking is not mentioned explicitly. TD also states that Third-Party Data is provided AS IS and that third-party providers are beneficiaries with enforcement rights (s.3.2). So whatever TD's licensed fields contain reflects an upstream vendor's rights, not independent facts.

A parity test must therefore be designed so TD output never enters the product. The researchers' suggested method is to source every value independently, fetch reference values only within TD's terms (for example a small internal QA sample on a paid plan, not stored beyond permitted timeframes), join on class ID or ticker plus valuation date, normalize units and rounding, report match rates by field with tolerance bands, classify mismatches as date lag, adjustment, mapping or true error, and adjudicate against the primary source rather than TD. Whether even that QA use is allowed is a question for counsel or written permission from TD. Vendor-proprietary fields should be excluded from parity targets.

## Prioritized recommendations

1. **Stop commercial use of Yahoo-sourced NAV and plan its replacement.** The data is personal-use and the terms bar income derivation (unverified second-hand quotes). It is also thin (6-30 points per fund). Keep it internal-only or remove it until a licensed source is in place.
2. **Open an NFN licensing track now.** Request the NFN Fund Data Price List, distributor agreement and data policy from Nasdaq Global Data Products, and extract the saved policy PDF locally to read the historical-data and licence-restriction sections. In parallel, request quotes with explicit API-redistribution rights and history depth from Xignite, dxFeed, Intrinio and ICE. Confirm NAV historical depth and redistribution in writing. This is the single biggest cost and the only route to a complete NAV history.
3. **Finish the free SEC profile build.** Ingest all RR monthly/quarterly/annual sets and the SCN file, take the latest filing per class, backfill the 2024 gap from EDGAR XBRL, add per-filing TSR extraction (expense ratio, net assets, holdings count, turnover, inception date), and ingest N-CEN flags. Store accession number and as-of date per value and show provenance. Respect 10 requests per second and a declared User-Agent. Compute the real coverage fraction against `company_tickers_mf.json` class IDs.
4. **Fill holdings from N-PORT and Rule 6c-11 ETF files.** Load the DERA N-PORT quarterly zips, then fetch newer NPORT-P filings per trust CIK via the submissions API. Show as-of dates. Add N-MFP for money market funds and N-CSR schedules or 10-K for commodity trusts. Build ETF daily-holdings connectors only after clearing each issuer's terms. Do not assume the Feb 2026 N-PORT proposal outcome.
5. **Do not redistribute CUSIP or ISIN until licensed.** Treat the SEC 13F cover-page restriction, as read directly, as controlling. Key the API on SEC class ID, ticker and FIGI (confirm OpenFIGI coverage and rate limits first). Approach CGS/FactSet for a vendor redistribution licence, and compare it against buying identifier-enriched data from the NFN reseller you choose. Treat a CGS contract as mandatory before shipping ISIN. Budget as unknown: no published redistributor price exists.
6. **Ship a derived, clearly labelled taxonomy instead of "category".** Build it from N-CEN flags, N-PORT asset mix, benchmark and objective text, with names that do not echo Morningstar or Lipper. Defer Morningstar or Lipper licences until revenue justifies a quote-only negotiation.
7. **Run a clean-room parity programme.** Define per-field tolerances and date conventions, adjudicate against primary sources, and ask TD for written permission or counsel's opinion before any QA sampling. Never load TD values into the product, and exclude category, ratings, risk statistics and ESG from parity claims.
8. **Position on provenance and price, not on matching TD.** The credible claims are full SEC-registered universe by series and class ID, filing-level lineage, explicit as-of dates and open redistribution rights, at a price well under TD's $999 Ultra tier. FMP already markets SEC-style holdings, so holdings alone are not a differentiator ([FMP](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/developer/docs/stable/mutual-fund-disclosures)).
9. **Verify every unconfirmed term before relying on it.** Read the live TD, Yahoo, Tiingo, Vanguard and iShares terms, the CGS licence-fees page and subscription agreement, the final FDTA rule text on instrument identifiers, and the current status of Dinosaur Financial v. S&P Global.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. Closing the data gaps is mostly a licensing and procurement exercise for three fields (identifiers, category, NAV history) and an engineering exercise for the rest. Profile and holdings fields are public facts available under a permissive SEC reuse regime, and what limits them is coverage, lag and parsing effort. The two expensive items, NFN-derived NAV and CGS identifiers, have no published prices, so budget and timeline are the largest unknowns. Matching TD is also a narrower claim than it first appears: for licensed fields, a match would only mean both vendors bought from the same upstream, and for proprietary fields no legitimate match exists. Because TD's terms bar the shortcut of copying its values, FundFlow's defensible edge is auditable sourcing at a lower price. Not legal advice; the unverified terms and the CGS and NFN contracts need counsel.
